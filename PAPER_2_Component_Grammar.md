The Voynich Manuscript as a Dual-Layer Functional and Instructional Notation
Component Grammar, Cross-Layer Convergence, and Independent Validation

Ronny Juul Nielsen
Independent Researcher
Denmark

Abstract

This paper develops a second information layer within a broader structural reconstruction of the Voynich Manuscript. Paper 1 established a comparatively stable glyph-level functional system, captured through EVA transcription, in which recurring manuscript categories behave as operators, states, transitions, and procedural markers within a recursive process grammar. The present study addresses a different question: what information is encoded within the internal construction of individual glyph occurrences?

Manuscript-first palaeographic analysis identifies a recurring component grammar based principally on C- and I-related structures together with connection topology, LINK architecture, orientation, position, scope, boundary realization, mirroring, embedding, and host relationships. These components are not interpreted as a lower alphabet whose elements combine semantically to generate EVA-level meanings. Instead, the evidence supports two distinct but mutually necessary information systems. The stable functional layer identifies WHAT operation or state is represented, whereas the component grammar specifies HOW that particular occurrence is internally instructed and realized.

Several independent observations support this distinction. Stable functional classes such as EVA d occur through multiple systematically different internal constructions, demonstrating that the same WHAT can admit multiple HOW realizations. Conversely, closely related component architectures can occur across different functional classes. The clearest cross-family case is provided by k, t, f, and p: the same upper-left C-component distinguishes k from t and f from p, while the two pairs differ through one versus two horizontal LINK relations. The same component contrast therefore recurs across two different EVA-level functional contrasts and cannot itself encode either resulting functional identity.

Additional convergence is provided by the internal architectures of y and n, the multiple d realizations, the C+C and I+I structures underlying ee and ii, transcription-witness disagreement patterns, and several key folios. In particular, f76r combines a detached functional reference sequence with an adjacent 28-unit demonstration block containing repeated controlled additions, deletions, substitutions, and embeddings, providing a manuscript-internal test of the functional model.

The resulting reconstruction treats the Voynich Manuscript as a layered technical process notation combining stable functional identity, variable instructional realization, recursive sequence grammar, and larger diagrammatic organization. These layers are complementary but non-isomorphic: neither the functional system nor the component system is semantically reducible to the other.

The paper concludes by defining explicit replication and falsification criteria. The model predicts that the same functional class may possess multiple instructional realizations and that different functional classes may possess closely related realizations, while genuinely distinct functional classes should not repeatedly exhibit completely identical full component constructions unless an additional informational variable remains unidentified, the functional classification requires revision, or the model itself is incomplete.

1. Introduction

The Voynich Manuscript has remained an exceptional challenge because its writing system exhibits both extreme regularity and extreme resistance to conventional linguistic interpretation. Previous approaches have primarily focused on the statistical properties of the glyph sequences, their positional behavior, and the possibility of identifying the manuscript’s underlying structure through transcription-based analysis.

In the first paper of this research framework (Nielsen, 2026a), the manuscript-level glyph categories represented in EVA were analyzed as a functional operator system rather than as a conventional alphabet. The resulting model proposed that these recurring glyph categories represent stable semantic-functional units participating in a recursive procedural grammar. Within this framework, individual EVA-level categories were interpreted as operators or states with consistent roles, including initiation, transformation, process, stabilization, transfer, output, and state marking.

The present paper extends this model by addressing a second fundamental question:

What information is encoded within the internal construction of the glyphs themselves?

The central proposal of this paper is that the Voynich notation contains two distinct but mutually necessary information systems:

A functional-semantic glyph layer, captured at the EVA level, which identifies what operation or state is represented.

An internal component grammar, based on recurring C/I structures and their relations, which specifies how that particular instance of the operation is instructed, organized, and realized.

The distinction is fundamental. The component grammar is not proposed as a lower alphabet from which the semantic identity of an EVA operator can simply be derived. Nor is the EVA-level category merely an abbreviation for the sum of its components.

Instead, the two layers encode different kinds of information.

At the functional level:

EVA specifies WHAT.

At the component level:

the internal construction instructs HOW.

The two systems are therefore complementary and mutually constraining, but not semantically reducible to one another.

For example, within the proposed model:

d represents transformation at the functional EVA level.

Individual manuscript realizations of d may contain substantially different internal component constructions.

These internal constructions need not independently “mean transformation.” Rather, they specify how a particular transformation instance is internally instructed or realized, while the stable glyph-level category identifies the operation as transformation.

Thus:

d = WHAT: transformation

while:

the internal component construction = HOW: the specific instruction or realization of this transformation

This distinction explains a central property of Voynich palaeography: manuscript glyphs can display considerable internal variation while retaining highly stable positional and functional behaviour.

A conventional EVA transcription successfully preserves much of this stable functional identity. However, because EVA necessarily compresses internal graphical variation, it does not preserve the complete instructional information contained within individual glyph constructions.

The proposed component model therefore investigates whether recurring primitive structures — principally C- and I-based forms together with connection geometry, positional scope, boundary realization, mirroring, and hybrid constructions — form a second systematic information layer within the manuscript.

The main hypothesis of this paper is therefore:

The Voynich notation contains two complementary encoding layers: a stable functional layer that identifies the operation or state, and a variable but rule-governed component layer that specifies the internal instruction or realization of each particular instance. Full interpretation requires both layers.

This hypothesis generates several testable predictions:

Glyph occurrences sharing the same functional EVA identity may contain systematically different internal component constructions.
Such component variation should be structured rather than arbitrary and should recur across independent manuscript locations.
The functional identity of an operator should remain stable despite variation in its internal realization.
Component structures should encode information not recoverable from EVA identity alone.
Special pages and diagrammatic structures should exhibit constructional principles compatible with the component grammar without requiring that their elements possess the same semantics as EVA operators.
Transcription disagreements should preferentially occur where component-level information is compressed, visually ambiguous, or difficult to represent linearly.

The resulting architecture is therefore not:

components → EVA meaning

and not:

EVA meaning → decorative component variation

but:

FUNCTIONAL LAYER

WHAT operation/state is this?

INSTRUCTIONAL COMPONENT LAYER

HOW is this particular instance instructed/realized?

=

complete procedural information

The analysis therefore proceeds from the proposal that the Voynich script is a layered graphical information system in which functional identity and internal instruction coexist as separate but symbiotic necessities.

The purpose of this paper is not to replace the EVA-level functional framework, nor to derive it from a lower graphical alphabet. It is to identify and reconstruct the second information system operating within the glyphs themselves.



2. Dual Inventory Framework: EVA Functional Inventory and C/I Component Grammar
2.1 From functional EVA analysis to a second information layer

The present paper continues the functional EVA model established in Paper 1.

The purpose of the additional analysis is not to replace, redefine, or derive the EVA functional inventory from smaller graphical elements.

Instead, the present analysis identifies a second information system operating within the manuscript glyphs.

The model therefore distinguishes two complementary inventories:

EVA Functional Inventory

The EVA-level inventory describes the stable functional identity of recurring manuscript glyph categories.

It answers:

What functional operator, state, or process role is represented?

Examples include:

d = transformation
t = transfer
q = active initiation
e = previously processed state
y = local completion
C/I Component Grammar

The C/I component grammar describes the internal architecture of a particular manuscript glyph occurrence.

It answers:

How is this particular instance of the operation internally instructed and realized?

The distinction is therefore:

FUNCTIONAL LAYER

WHAT is this operation/state?

↓

stable glyph identity
represented at EVA level

and independently:

INSTRUCTIONAL COMPONENT LAYER

HOW is this particular instance
to be carried out?

↓

variable internal component construction

These are not competing descriptions, but neither is one simply a semantic decomposition of the other.

The component construction does not need to “spell” or independently encode the EVA operator identity.

A particular d-construction, for example, does not need to contain components whose individual meanings combine to produce “transformation.”

The EVA-level glyph identity already specifies:

d = transformation

while the internal construction specifies the particular instructional realization of that transformation.

The complete manuscript glyph therefore carries two complementary forms of information:

2.4 The C/I Component Grammar

The component model identifies two fundamental recurring graphical families:

C

and:

I

These are not interpreted as alphabetic letters beneath EVA.

Nor should they be treated as smaller units whose meanings mechanically combine to generate EVA meanings.

They constitute the basic graphical vocabulary of the second, instructional layer.

They were identified as fundamental because they:

occur independently,
recur across glyph families,
combine recursively,
participate in compound structures,
and generate a large proportion of observed internal glyph architecture.

The component grammar therefore contains:

primitive component forms,
relational geometry,
positional transformations,
scope,
orientation,
and compound constructions.

Its function is to describe the internal instruction encoded by a specific glyph realization.

2.6 Relations are not independent primitives

Several recurring features are better understood as relationships between component structures rather than as additional primitives.

LINK

It describes connection geometry between component structures.

Two important realizations are:

C—LINK—C

LINK2

The distinction is topological.

Two constructions are particularly important:

ee

and:

ii

They are exceptional because the distinction between EVA-level repetition and component-level repetition remains unusually transparent.

At EVA level:

Corpus analysis shows that e and i are exceptional in their ability to form extended repeated sequences:

This behaviour differs from most other EVA functional categories.

At the functional level, the two sequences represent different procedural dimensions.

At the component level, they expose repeated C- and I-based structures in unusually pure form.

They therefore provide an important interface between the two systems without implying that the systems are semantically identical.

2.10 Preliminary C/I refinement of g and m

At the EVA functional level, the current interpretation remains:

g / m = result/consolidation family

This functional relationship was established independently of the later C/I analysis.

Component comparison subsequently revealed a strong structural parallel in which the two result-family forms differ principally through C- versus I-dominant internal realization.

The present working hypothesis is therefore:

g = C-dominant instructional realization
within the result/consolidation family

m = I-dominant instructional realization
within the result/consolidation family

This does not mean:

C = g
I = m

nor does it imply that C and I independently encode the semantic concept “result.”

Instead, the EVA layer identifies both as members of the result/consolidation functional domain, while the component layer may distinguish how those result instances are internally instructed or realized.

This remains a working refinement requiring independent component-level validation.

2.11 The symbiotic dual-system model

The resulting framework consists of two mutually necessary but semantically non-redundant systems.

Functional EVA-level system

Defines:

WHAT operation or state is represented?

Examples:

d = transformation
t = transfer
q = active initiation
e = processed prior state
y = local completion

This layer is comparatively stable.

C/I instructional component system

Defines:

HOW is this particular instance
internally instructed and realized?

It records information including:

component selection,
component order,
linkage,
orientation,
scope,
boundary realization,
embedding,
and internal pathway.

This layer may vary substantially between occurrences belonging to the same EVA functional category.

The two systems should therefore not be represented as a simple hierarchy such as:

C/I components
↓
EVA meaning

Instead, the manuscript occurrence contains both:

                       │
            ┌──────────┴──────────┐
            │                     │
   FUNCTIONAL IDENTITY    INSTRUCTIONAL REALIZATION
            │                     │
          WHAT                   HOW

The layers are symbiotic because neither supplies the complete information carried by the glyph.

The functional layer tells the reader which operation or state is involved.

The component layer specifies how that particular instance is internally organized, instructed, or realized.

A component construction may therefore change without changing the EVA-level operator.

Conversely, knowing the component architecture alone does not necessarily identify the EVA semantic class.

The central conclusion is:

The EVA-level functional inventory and the C/I component grammar encode different but complementary information. EVA specifies WHAT operation or state is represented; the component grammar instructs HOW that particular instance is to be carried out or instantiated. Neither layer is semantically reducible to the other, and complete interpretation requires both.




3. Discovery Pathway and Independent Convergence of the Functional and Component Systems
3.1 Independent discovery paths

A central methodological feature of the present analysis is that the functional EVA-level model and the C/I component grammar emerged through substantially different analytical pathways.

The functional model was initially developed through analysis of:

corpus-wide distribution,
positional behaviour,
recurring sequences,
minimal contrasts,
controlled additions and removals of EVA-level operators,
and relationships between repeated functional structures.

This analysis addressed:

WHAT functional operation or state is represented?

The later component grammar emerged independently through:

palaeographic comparison,
direct glyph decomposition,
recurring graphical structures,
diagrammatic analysis,
spatial relationships,
and comparison between written and visual constructions.

This analysis addressed a different question:

HOW is a particular occurrence internally instructed and realized?

The methodological importance of this distinction is that the component system was not introduced in order to derive the EVA semantics.

The EVA functions already existed as independently established hypotheses before the internal component architecture was reconstructed.

Likewise, the component grammar was not inferred by decomposing the previously assigned EVA meanings into smaller semantic units.

The two systems emerged from different evidence.

Their later correspondence is therefore a convergence between independent descriptive layers.

3.2 First discovery stage: functional EVA behaviour

The initial analysis focused on recurring EVA-level categories without assuming that they represented ordinary alphabetic writing.

The principal observation was that the categories displayed systematic behaviour:

recurring positional preferences,
stable combinations,
predictable transitions,
repeated functional contrasts,
and recursive sequence relationships.

These observations suggested that the recurring manuscript categories captured by EVA behave as functional operators and states rather than ordinary letters.

Examples include:

d = transformation
t = transfer
q = active initiation
e = previously processed state
y = locally completed processed state

These interpretations were derived from functional and sequence behaviour.

They did not depend on later C/I decomposition.

The first analytical system can therefore be represented as:

functional contrasts
            ↓
stable EVA-level categories
            ↓

3.3 Second discovery stage: diagrammatic construction principles

A separate analytical pathway began with the manuscript's visual structures.

Rather than treating the imagery as decorative material surrounding an independent text, recurring graphical forms were compared across:

The analysis revealed recurring constructional families involving:

The important observation was not that these visual forms directly represented EVA operators.

Nor was it that each graphical primitive possessed the same semantics at diagram and glyph level.

Rather, the manuscript appeared to employ a restricted and recurrent visual construction logic across different scales.

This independent diagrammatic investigation eventually directed attention inward, toward the construction of the written glyphs themselves.

Instead, the diagrammatic evidence suggested that the manuscript repeatedly constructs information through combinations of:

This observation became important because the same kinds of graphical relations later appeared inside the written glyphs.

The diagrammatic analysis therefore did not decode the glyphs.

It revealed the possibility that a second graphical information system existed beneath the EVA-level functional categories.

4. Empirical Demonstration of the Dual Information Model
4.1 Selection of validation cases

The dual information model is evaluated against manuscript locations where the distinction between stable functional identity and variable internal instruction can be examined particularly clearly.

The selected cases are not treated as isolated visual correspondences.

They were selected because they contain one or more of the following properties:

repeated functional structures,
controlled contrasts,
systematic glyph variation,
unusually exposed component architecture,
internal page organization,
and independent relationships between textual and diagrammatic structures.

The principal demonstration cases are:

f57v — controlled variation within recurring glyph families,
f18r — direct palaeographic analysis of internally different functional operators,
f49v — controlled EVA-level contrasts and component organization,
f66r — sequential and key-like organization,
f69r — radial diagrammatic organization,
f76r — internal correspondence between a vertical reference sequence and an adjacent 28-line demonstration section,
Rosettes — large-scale recursive diagrammatic organization.

Together, these cases allow the functional and instructional layers to be examined from different directions.

The central empirical distinction is:

8. Reinterpreting Previous Approaches: From Alphabetic Assumptions to Layered Functional Reconstruction
8.1 The central methodological shift

Previous approaches to the Voynich Manuscript have often begun from an alphabetic model:

glyph
↓
letter
↓
word
↓
language

Under this assumption, the principal question becomes:

Which known or unknown language do the glyph sequences encode?

The present reconstruction begins from a different observation.

Recurring manuscript glyph categories display stable positional, combinatorial, and procedural behaviour that is more naturally described in functional terms.

At the same time, direct palaeographic analysis shows that individual occurrences of those stable categories can contain substantial internal graphical variation.

The relevant questions therefore separate into two levels:

FUNCTIONAL LEVEL

WHAT operation or state
is represented?

and:

INSTRUCTIONAL COMPONENT LEVEL

HOW is this particular instance
internally instructed and realized?

A third level concerns the organization of these structures across larger manuscript diagrams.

The methodological shift is therefore not simply:

language
→
graphics

or:

glyph
→
components
→
meaning

It is a shift from alphabetic substitution toward reconstruction of several interacting information systems.

8.2 Why EVA was necessary — and why it remains fundamental

EVA has been indispensable to Voynich research because it provides a standardized abstraction through which recurring manuscript forms can be compared computationally.

Without such a transcriptional system:

corpus-wide comparison would be extremely difficult,
positional regularities would be harder to identify,
sequence behaviour could not be measured efficiently,
and functional contrasts would be much harder to reconstruct.

The present model therefore does not treat EVA as an obstacle to be discarded.

On the contrary, the analysis suggests that EVA has been successful because it captures a genuinely stable level of the manuscript system.

More precisely:

EVA provides a modern transcriptional representation of a stable manuscript-level functional glyph inventory.

EVA itself is modern.

The functional layer it captures is a property of the manuscript.

The distinction is important.

The present analysis does not propose that the manuscript author used “EVA.”

It proposes that the recurring glyph categories represented by EVA correspond closely enough to a real functional level of the original notation that their systematic behaviour can be reconstructed independently.

Thus:

EVA-LEVEL CATEGORY
=
stable functional identity
=
WHAT

However, EVA necessarily compresses many internal graphical distinctions.

It generally preserves:

which functional glyph family?

better than:

what exact internal instruction
does this particular realization contain?

The limitation of EVA is therefore not that it identifies the wrong level.

It is that it represents one level extremely efficiently while necessarily losing information from another.

8.3 Why multiple transcription witnesses do not automatically solve the problem

The existence of several independent EVA witnesses is valuable because disagreement identifies uncertain manuscript locations.

However:

more witnesses
≠
automatically greater structural resolution

Several witnesses may independently attempt to represent the same manuscript construction within a transcription system that does not preserve all of its internal detail.

Consequently, witness plurality can reveal uncertainty without necessarily resolving the underlying construction.

This is demonstrated particularly clearly by the f76r analysis, where direct manuscript retranscription recovered functional relationships obscured or fragmented across existing witness readings.

The later component model extends the same principle further.

A transcription can correctly identify the broad functional category while still omitting information encoded inside the glyph occurrence.

Thus the relevant distinction is:

transcription accuracy

versus:

transcription resolution

A transcription may be accurate at the functional level while remaining incomplete at the instructional component level.

8.4 The limitation of treating glyphs as indivisible signs

The component analysis shows that many manuscript glyph occurrences cannot adequately be described as indivisible graphical units.

They contain recurring internal structures involving:

The traditional simplifying assumption:

=

This distinction explains why:

apparently similar forms can differ internally,
particular glyph families are disproportionately difficult to transcribe,
and EVA-level regularity can coexist with substantial palaeographic complexity.

8.10 System stability through layered encoding

The dual information architecture also provides an explanation for another striking property of the Voynich notation: the relative stability of its functional system across the manuscript despite extensive local variation.

If every procedural distinction required a new functional glyph, the notation would be expected to expand continuously as additional procedures, materials, states, and transformations were introduced.

A stable functional category can be retained while its internal instructional realization changes.

The functional layer can remain conservative because procedural specificity does not need to be expressed through continual invention of new functional signs.

9. Concluding Perspective

The present analysis concerns primarily the internal architecture of the Voynich Manuscript: its functional glyph system, instructional component grammar, procedural sequence behaviour, quantitative regularities, transcription structure, and diagrammatic organization.

The reconstruction distinguishes three interacting information systems:

WHAT
=
stable functional identity

HOW
=
instance-specific internal instruction

ORGANIZATION
=
larger procedural and diagrammatic structure

Their combination provides an economical notation in which a relatively stable functional inventory can support extensive procedural variation and high information density without requiring correspondingly long linear sequences or continual expansion of the visible functional system.

Questions concerning the manuscript's original users, precise historical purpose, transmission, ownership, and broader cultural setting remain separate historical problems.

If the reconstruction is correct, the manuscript is compatible with a structured technical or pedagogical process notation rather than ordinary prose. However, the precise historical purpose cannot be established solely from the internal reconstruction and should not be conflated with it.

The principal contribution of the present work is therefore the reconstruction of the manuscript's internal notation architecture and the production of explicit structures that can be independently tested.

EVA-level frequency

with:

component-resolved frequency

without assuming that one replaces the other.

This will allow direct tests of questions such as:

how many EVA-identical occurrences contain distinct internal realizations?
how many witness disagreements correspond to reproducible component differences?
which component distinctions are functionally neutral?
which instructional distinctions correlate with sequence position?
and how much information is lost by conventional linear transcription?

C.19 Summary

The quantitative evidence operates through two independently testable routes.

At the functional level:

WHAT

is tested through corpus and sequence behaviour.

At the component/witness level:

HOW

is tested through manuscript structure and the historical distribution of transcription ambiguity.

The current evidence includes:

highly stable ee/ii near-equilibrium,
high-frequency coherent edy and dy structures,
3,416 clean witness-disagreement positions,
strong concentration within particular glyph families,
extreme contextual concentration in several major disagreement classes,
and a marked association between apparent grammatical anomalies and transcription instability.

The correct interpretation is not:

statistics prove the model

but:

Several independently derived structural distinctions produce measurable and unexpectedly convergent quantitative behaviour.

The scientific test is therefore whether independent researchers can reproduce those relationships under transparent corpus definitions and whether alternative models can explain the same combined pattern with equal or greater predictive power.

F.1 Purpose and scope

This appendix defines a protocol for independent testing of the proposed Voynich notation model.

The purpose is not to provide additional interpretation of manuscript material already used to construct the model.

The purpose is to determine whether researchers who were not involved in its development can reproduce its principal structures from the manuscript itself.

The model contains three analytically distinct systems:

=

INSTRUCTIONAL COMPONENT SYSTEM
=
HOW?

and:

=

These systems generate different predictions and must therefore initially be tested separately.

The core validation principle is:

Independent researchers should be able to apply the published framework to material not previously solved for them and determine whether the same system emerges.

External validation should therefore not consist merely of checking whether selected examples appear compatible with the published interpretation.

It should require independent reconstruction.
